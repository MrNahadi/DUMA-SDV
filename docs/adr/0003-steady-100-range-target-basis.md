# 0003. Steady-100 km/h range target stays at 510 km, re-based on matched-condition data; the loss parameters are calibrated to the real car

Status: accepted (supersedes ADR 0001 row 25 basis, the ADR 0001 consequence on range-test conditions, and the ADR 0001 values in rows 17 and 20 used as defaults)
Decided-by: answerer (researched)
Question: PH1-Q1 push-back round 1. With ADR 0001's own parameters (2,130 kg, Crr 0.008, CdA 0.504 m², battery-to-wheel efficiency 0.85, 0.4 kW aux), steady 100 km/h works out to about 135 Wh/km and about 610 km. Is the 510 km (162 Wh/km) target inconsistent, and should row 25 be re-derived for the stated test conditions (flat, 20-25 °C, HVAC off)?
Decision: Keep the target at **510 km at a steady 100 km/h (band 459-561 km, about 162 Wh/km from the battery)**. Drop the Highmotor leg of the derivation, because its conditions (10-15 °C, hilly motorway) do not match the test. The target now rests on EV Database's mild-highway figure, whose conditions match the test, plus official and ADAC-measured cross-checks. These show the real car uses about 20-30% more energy than ADR 0001's idealised loss parameters predict. The simulator must be calibrated to the real car, not the target lowered to fit idealised parameters.

Basis:
- **Measured steady-speed data for this exact car:** none found for the RWD 82.5 kWh car at 90-110 km/h in mild weather. The Bjørn Nyland Seal figures in circulation (200 Wh/km at 90 km/h, 236 Wh/km at 120 km/h) come from a car labelled "Performance" (2024). That label is the dual-motor AWD car in several markets, and the test temperature is not published, so I discarded them. Source: https://www.autohled.cz/de/a/byd/seal. ADAC gives no constant-speed figure. It says only that ranges "über 500 km" are possible when driving calmly "möglichst unter 100 km/h": https://assets.adac.de/image/upload/Autodatenbank/Autotest/at6432-byd-seal-design/byd-seal-design.pdf
- **Primary anchor, conditions matched:** EV Database's "Mild weather" is defined as "23 °C and no use of A/C", with "a constant speed of 110 km/h" for Highway. That matches the test (flat, 20-25 °C, HVAC off) except for the speed. It gives 455 km at 181 Wh/km, battery-side, since 455 × 0.181 ≈ 82.4 kWh. Sources: https://ev-database.org/car/3515/BYD-SEAL-825-kWh-RWD-Design and https://ev-database.org/uk/car/2001/BYD-SEAL-825-kWh-RWD-Design. Scaling from 110 to 100 km/h by road load gives a ratio of 0.896 with idealised parameters, or 0.907 with calibrated ones. That is 162-164 Wh/km, or 502-509 km.
- **The EV Database methodology agrees with first-principles physics on a known-efficient car.** The same idealised model (1,835 kg, Crr 0.008, CdA 0.219 × 2.22, η 0.85, 0.4 kW aux) gives 139.7 Wh/km at 110 km/h for a Tesla Model 3 RWD (Highland). EV Database lists 138 Wh/km for that car under the same mild-highway definition: https://ev-database.org/car/3403/Tesla-Model-3-RWD. So the Seal's 181 Wh/km is not a methodology artefact. It reflects the Seal specifically.
- **Independent evidence that the Seal uses 20-30% more energy than a Model 3:**
  - ADAC Ecotest (same protocol, measured, including charging losses): Seal Design 21.2 kWh/100 km vs Model 3 17.2-17.3 kWh/100 km, a ratio of about 1.23. Sources: the ADAC PDF above, and https://www.arenaev.com/peugeot_e208_e308_corsa_electric_top_adacs_ecotest_for_2024-news-4344.php
  - Official WLTP battery-side consumption: Seal 145 Wh/km vs Model 3 RWD 112 Wh/km, a ratio of about 1.29 (EV Database pages above).
  - Idealised physics predicts only about 1.08 from mass and CdA alone. The rest is losses the idealised parameters leave out: drivetrain and inverter efficiency, and the real rolling resistance of the 19-inch tyres at about 2.1 t.
- **Sensitivity:** one owner report of about 17 kWh/100 km at a 110 km/h cruise in mild weather (https://www.bitauto.com/wiki/10034941883/) scales to about 540 km at 100 km/h. That is still inside the 459-561 km band.
- **The push-back's physics is right about the idealised model:** it gives about 135 Wh/km, or about 612 km. The brief sets the target as the reference car's figures (brief §4: "within ±10% of the reference car's published figures"), and ranks correctness first (§10). The real-car evidence therefore wins, and the idealised loss parameters are what change.

Consequences:
- Supersedes ADR 0001's consequence on range-test conditions. The range reference test runs at a steady 100 km/h, flat road, 23 °C (20-25 °C allowed), HVAC off, 0.4 kW auxiliary load, from 100% to 0% of usable energy (82.5 kWh). It asserts 459-561 km.
- Supersedes ADR 0001's default values for rows 17 and 20, and adds an efficiency default. Official rows (Cd 0.219, mass, tyres, power, torque) stay fixed. The simulator's loss terms, all of them E rows, are calibrated so that the steady 110 km/h result lands near EV Database's 181 Wh/km (±5%). Starting defaults that do this: Crr 0.011, frontal area 2.35 m² (CdA ≈ 0.515 m²), and combined gear × inverter × motor efficiency of about 0.82 at highway part-load. These give 179 Wh/km at 110 km/h and 163 Wh/km (507 km) at 100 km/h. Record any different calibration in a new ADR.
- Calibration aid, not a new pass/fail target: with the calibrated values, the simulator's WLTC range should come out roughly near the official 570 km (145 Wh/km battery-side). A large miss means the loss split between rolling, aero and drivetrain is wrong, even if the 100 km/h test passes.
- The 0-100 km/h (5.9 s) and DC 10-80% (37 min) targets from ADR 0001 are unchanged. Raising Crr adds about 60 N, which slows 0-100 by less than 0.05 s and stays inside the band.
- ADR 0001 stays as written. Its row 25 and range-test consequence carry a pointer to this ADR.
