# 05 · Faults and diagnostics: requirements

All requirements remain unverified until the feature tickets and validation pass. Diagnostic calibration and recovery rules are to be recorded in an ADR before implementation.

1. **R1 — Injection.** Through the public sim API and Diagnostics view, a user can inject cell over-temperature, insulation fault, motor over-temperature and 12 V low. Each choice has a stable identity, responsible ECU and affected `CarPart`; invalid or duplicate actions have a deterministic, visible outcome.
2. **R2 — DTC lifecycle.** Each injected condition raises a coded DTC from its responsible ECU. The diagnostics snapshot distinguishes active from stored records. An active DTC cannot be cleared while its condition remains abnormal; once the condition returns to normal it becomes stored and can be cleared. Repeated ticks or repeat injection do not create duplicate records. Clearing the log requires explicit confirmation and cancellation preserves it.
3. **R3 — Bus provenance.** Fault status and control effects pass through catalogue-declared ECU CAN messages with unique IDs, senders, cadence/event designation, units and scaling. The VCU and IC respond to received frames, including defined stale-frame behavior, not another ECU's private state. The existing `trace()` exposes the fault-related traffic for phase 06.
4. **R4 — Driving response.** Cell over-temperature during READY driving produces an amber plain-language dashboard warning, a BMS DTC, a lower VCU-authorized power limit and measurably reduced drive power under the same pedal/speed conditions. At least one severe fault invokes a distinct limp mode with a stronger cap while preserving controlled movement where safe; the ADR specifies exact policy. Normal behavior returns only after recovery, subject to the stored log remaining.
5. **R5 — Safety and charging.** The ADR specifies how each fault affects startup, contactors, traction, regen and AC/DC charging. Unsafe paths refuse or stop energy transfer; clearing records alone never bypasses an active condition or stale authorization. Fault-free charging and driving behavior remains unchanged.
6. **R6 — Driver and diagnostic display.** Drive shows a short plain-language warning, severity and any derate/limp state without requiring DTC knowledge. Diagnostics is the single place for codes, active/stored status, affected module and actions. Stale diagnostic data shows unavailable rather than a false healthy state. Status is never conveyed by colour alone.
7. **R7 — Car location.** An active fault reveals and highlights its mapped module on the procedural x-ray car using `--warn` or `--fault` as appropriate, with a visible module name. Multiple active faults have a deterministic selection/priority rule. Clearing the active condition removes that highlight without affecting wheel, lighting or charge-port states.
8. **R8 — Interaction and design.** Controls are keyboard reachable and use the exact labels **Inject fault**, **Clear fault**, **Clear all faults**. A centered confirmation modal explains the consequence of clearing all; cancellation changes nothing, confirmation shows completion feedback. The Diagnostics view follows `DESIGN-RULES.md` at 1366×768 with no horizontal scroll and appropriate empty, loading and error/unavailable states.
9. **R9 — Determinism and regressions.** A headless fault-during-driving scenario repeats with identical snapshots and trace. Existing startup, gear, speed, regen, charge, 0–100 km/h, steady 100 km/h range and 10–80% DC tests continue to pass at their existing bounds. The sim remains framework-free, fixed at 10 ms with SI internals and no new packages.
